Find an NVLAP-accredited lab that can analyze asbestos — then a licensed contractor if the report says to remove it.

You cannot tell asbestos by looking. EPA says a trained professional should take samples for laboratory analysis. Start with a lab accredited under NIST’s National Voluntary Laboratory Accreditation Program for Asbestos Fiber Analysis. Hire a licensed abatement contractor only after the numbers, and only if the report (and a qualified inspector) says the material should be repaired or removed.

1. Analyze

Use an NVLAP-accredited lab

NIST’s Asbestos Fiber Analysis program accredits labs to analyze samples with polarized light microscopy (PLM) and/or transmission electron microscopy (TEM). AHERA requires NVLAP accreditation for labs that analyze samples from public or private elementary or secondary schools. How to search NVLAP.

2. Read the report

Leave it, repair it, or remove it

EPA: asbestos-containing material in good condition that will not be disturbed is not likely to pose a health risk. Usually the best thing is to leave it alone. Repair or removal is for material that is more than slightly damaged, or that a remodel would disturb. When to test vs. when to hire.

3. Hire only if needed

A licensed contractor, not a ranking

EPA: use two different firms so the inspector who assesses the need for work is not connected to the firm that does the repair or removal. This site lists state licensees as the state printed them. We do not rank abatement companies.
